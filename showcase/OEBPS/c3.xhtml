Chapter 3. Snow

Snow fell in the night without a sound, and in the morning the island was a page that nobody had written on. The yard was white, the wall was white, the path to the landing was a long white question. Even the sea looked colder, grey and heavy under a low sky, and the snowflakes kept coming, small and dry, drifting sideways past the lantern glass.

Ada went out in her sister's gloves to clear the steps. Her breath hung in front of her. The snow squeaked under her boots and the frost had made lace of the gorse; every blade of grass by the wall stood up in its own coat of ice.

She worked until her shoulders ached, then stood at the top of the steps and looked at what she had done: a dark line through the white, from her door to the edge of the world.

In the afternoon the snow stopped. The clouds pulled back from the west and a thin cold sun came out, low over the water, and turned the drifts gold and blue. Ada made tea and sat by the window with the logbook, and wrote: Snow, four inches. Wind light, northerly. Sea moderate. Steps cleared. Then, after a moment, in smaller letters: Saturday.

The lamp went on at four. In the frozen dark the beam went round and round over the snow, and the snow gave it back, so that for a few hours the whole island seemed to glow.
