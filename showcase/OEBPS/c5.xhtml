Chapter 5. High Summer

By June the sun stood high over the island all day, and the sea lay blue and still around it, and the grass on the headland had gone the colour of bread. Ada worked in the sunshine with her sleeves rolled up, painting the gallery rail, and the paint dried almost as fast as she could lay it on. The light was everywhere: on the water, on the white walls, in the brass of the lantern, so bright at noon that she had to work with her eyes half closed against the glare.

Tobias came out on the Thursday with the summer stores and the tide tables for the season, which the board sent every year in a brown envelope as if they were state secrets.

"Read me the springs," Ada said, wiping her hands.

He read them off the card in the warm wind, squinting in the sunlight:

DateHigh waterHeightLow waterHeightMoon
June 1406:125.4 m12:310.6 mFull
June 1506:585.6 m13:150.4 mWaning
June 2805:405.2 m11:520.8 mNew

"The full moon in the middle of June," she said. "Good. The rocks will be dry at noon. I'll scrape the landing."

"You could come into the village instead. There's a fair."

"There's always a fair."

"There's never been one with me at it before," he said, and she laughed, and the sun was hot on her shoulders, and the summer seemed, for a moment, as if it would go on forever.

They ate their lunch on the warm rocks by the landing, bread and cheese and the last of the damson jam, with their feet in the shallows and the noon light dancing on the water.
