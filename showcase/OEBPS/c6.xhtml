Chapter 6. The Gale

The wind got up in the afternoon. It came out of the south-west, warm and restless at first, pushing at the long grass, flattening the gorse, setting the washing on the line cracking like gunshots. By teatime it was a gale. It howled round the tower and boomed in the chimney, and every gust shook the windows in their frames.

Ada brought the washing in and lashed the shutters and went up to the lantern early. From the gallery she could see the whole western sky turning the colour of a bruise, and under it the sea, white from the reef to the horizon, every wave torn into spray by the wind.

Then the storm broke. The rain came first, flung sideways, hammering on the glass. Then the thunder, so close she felt it in the iron of the stair. Lightning split the dark over the reef and for one white instant she saw everything: the rocks, the broken water, and out beyond the point, where no boat should have been, a small boat with a single light, climbing a wave as tall as a house.

She knew the boat. She would have known it in her sleep.

She did not remember coming down the stair. She remembered the lamp, swinging in her hand; the door ripped out of her grip by the gale; the rain like thrown gravel; the path to the landing running like a river. She remembered standing at the end of the landing in the screaming wind, holding the lamp up as high as she could reach, and the tower behind her throwing its beam out over the storm, round and round, steady, as if the whole island were holding up a light.

The boat came in on the back of a breaker. It struck the landing, hard, and Tobias jumped, and missed, and caught the edge, and Ada caught him.

"You fool," she said, when she could say anything. "You utter fool. In this storm--"

"You said you wouldn't come to the fair," he shouted over the thunder. "So I brought the fair to you."

He opened his oilskin. Inside, soaked through, was a paper bag of sugared almonds and a blue ribbon.

She laughed until she cried, or cried until she laughed; in the rain it was impossible to tell. Then she took his hand, and they went up together through the gale to the tower, and the light went round above them all night, and in the morning the wind dropped, and the sea lay down, and the sun came up on a washed and shining world.
