Chapter 2. Fog, and Then the Sea

The fog came in on the Tuesday, slow and white, out of a sea so flat it seemed to have forgotten how to move. First the far headland went, then the buoy, then the end of the landing, until the whole world was the tower and a few yards of wet grass and the sound of the foghorn going every thirty seconds, low and patient, into the mist.

Ada kept the horn all day and all night. She ate standing up. Twice she thought she heard an engine out in the haze, and twice it was only the blood in her ears. Fog does that: it fills your head with the shapes of things that are not there, and hides the things that are.

On the Wednesday morning there was a letter under the door of the oil store, damp at the corners, folded twice. She knew the hand before she opened it.

The Landing, Wednesday.

Dear Ada,

I came out at first light with the post but could not find the island, which is a poor thing for a skipper to admit. I heard your horn and steered on it and found the landing by walking into it. I am leaving this where the damp won't take it, and the paraffin by the door. Saturday still stands. The fog will lift; it always does, and when it lifts you will see the sea as it was before, only washed.

Yours, in the haze,
Tobias Reed

She read it twice and put it in the drawer with the logbook, which was where she kept the things that mattered.

* * *

By noon the fog was thinning. It did not lift so much as come apart, in long pale rags that drifted over the grass and caught in the gorse, and then, all at once, the sea was there again.

It was not flat any more. A long swell was coming in from the west, slow and enormous, the waves rising green and glassy and breaking white along the rocks below the tower. The spray climbed the cliff and hung in the air and fell back. Out beyond the reef the whole ocean was moving, row behind row, the tide turning under it, and the light on the water was so bright after two days of mist that Ada had to shade her eyes.

A boat was coming round the point, riding the swell, rising and dropping out of sight and rising again. She watched it all the way to the shore.
