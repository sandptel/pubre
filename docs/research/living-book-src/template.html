Part I · One visual language

Every mark means one thing, everywhere

Before adding features, pubre needs a grammar. Each typographic channel carries exactly one kind of meaning, the same in the guide, the lenses, your highlights and the capsule. Learn it once and the page explains itself. Motion follows the same rule: each meaning has one verb, and it only plays where your cursor is.

ChannelMeansLooks likeMotion at the cursor
Ink colourWho. A person or a speaking voice. Each character keeps one base16 hue for the whole book. Ahab, Starbuck, Stubb · “Sing out for him!”
UnderlineTell me more. Hover or tap for help. The line’s style says what kind. spleen definition · Nantucket term or place · rope-yarn note
Coloured washYours. Highlights, one colour per meaning (renamable). The system never uses a wash.
PointerThe word you’re on. Whole-word by default; underline, dot or outline if you prefer. Some years ago
Weight & slantYour emphasis. Bold and italic are only ever yours. The author’s own italics are never changed. bold (a stroke, no reflow) · italic Stillemphasis never moves
ContrastAttention & time. Your sentence is full ink, its paragraph softer, the page quieter, and what you’ve read quietest. Asides whisper one step down. focus · paragraph · page · read
Margin markNotes & structure. A dot for a note, a tick at a chapter end, a ribbon for a bookmark. Call me Ishmael.

Rules

  • One channel, one meaning. A word can carry several channels (a name inside your highlight, under the cursor), but no channel ever means two things.
  • The system never takes your channels. Coloured washes, bold and italic are reserved for you.
  • Motion only at the cursor, or where you just acted. Nothing else on the page moves. Each animation plays once, under 450 ms, and reduced motion gets a still equivalent.
  • All colour comes from base16 slots, so every palette and mode keeps the same grammar.

Highlight meanings

Six colours, matching the W3C annotation vocabulary so exports stay portable. Rename them freely; the colour keeps its slot.

One intensity control

Instead of a dozen toggles, one setting in Themes & Settings:

  • Quiet: only yours, the pointer and contrast.
  • Guided: adds who and tell-me-more. This is the default.
  • Rich: adds every motion verb, sparks and the living capsule.
Part II · The reading guide

A finger that waits for you

A pointer sits on the word you’re on, the whole word by default, and hops to the next one with a little spring. Contrast does the focusing: your sentence is full ink, the rest of its paragraph softer, the page quieter, and what you’ve read quietest. It never runs away. Hold Space and it glides at your pace; let go and it stops. Tap to step one word; double-tap to jump back to the start of the sentence. Arrow keys and vim motions move by word, sentence and paragraph. Try it: click the passage, then use the keys.

Bindings follow Vimium’s caret mode and editor conventions. On phones, a thumb pad does the same: hold to glide, tap to step, double-tap for the sentence start, swipe for sentences and paragraphs.

Why it won’t run away

Auto-scrolling pacers fail because they keep going while your mind wanders, and getting back costs more than reading slower. Here motion needs your input. Holding is a dead-man’s switch, so the moment you look away and lift your thumb, it waits.

Speed isn’t a setting you hunt for. Your taps teach it: the gap between taps, weighted by word length and punctuation, becomes your pace, and glide uses it. The same pace powers every “min left” in pubre.

Open questions for you

  • Double-tap vs. fast tapping. At about 240 wpm a word lasts about 250 ms, so quick stepping taps can look like a double-tap. The demo steps immediately and, if a second tap lands within 260 ms, undoes that step and jumps to the sentence start, so there’s no wait. Alternatives: keep only ( for this and turn double-tap off, or only count a double-tap when it’s clearly faster than your learned tap rhythm.
  • Step unit and focus style: both are switchable in the demo above (Word / Phrase; Sentence / Line focus / Paragraph). Which should be the default?
  • Space while reading normally turns the page. Is a G toggle for guide mode right, or would you rather hold a different key (for example F) so Space keeps paging?

What the research says

  • Adults read fiction at about 260 wpm (most between 175 and 300). That is the guide’s starting pace before it learns yours. Brysbaert 2019
  • Blocking look-backs hurts comprehension, which is why RSVP speed-readers fail. Going back has to stay one keypress away. Schotter et al. 2014
  • Short lines raised dyslexic readers’ speed 27%, halved regressions and kept comprehension the same. That supports Line focus. Schneps et al. 2013
  • No adult study shows a moving pointer improves comprehension. So the guide is a focus and place-keeping aid, not a speed-reading trick, and “dim what’s read” is off by default.

In the real reader

  • When the cursor reaches the last word on the page, the page turns and the cursor carries on.
  • Leaving the guide keeps your exact word (stored as a CFI) as your reading position.
  • The cursor is drawn over the page, not inside the book’s text, and tints are 1–3 ranges at a time, so it stays fast even with Safari’s highlight slowdown bug.
  • Pauses at commas, full stops and paragraph ends feel like breathing.
  • With reduced motion, the cursor jumps between words instead of sliding.
  • Cards never interrupt: definitions and notes appear only after you rest on a word for about 0.6 s, and vanish as soon as you move.
  • Later: with text-to-speech or a book’s own narration, the cursor follows the voice.
Part III · Word lenses & highlights

Words that explain themselves

Lenses tint certain words and explain them on hover or tap. Names get their character colour. Hard words (rare in English, Zipf below 3) get a dotted underline. Speech takes the speaker’s colour. Asides whisper a step quieter. Foreign phrases get a dashed underline. Select any text to highlight it in a colour that carries a meaning, add your own bold or italic, and attach a note that shows on hover. All of it follows the grammar in Part I.

Passage
Intensity

Try the three passages and the intensity levels, and watch the counter: what doesn’t fit the budget is held back, not lost. Hover any word to reveal held marks. Select a phrase to highlight it; each colour has a meaning (Key, Beautiful, Idea, Feeling, Question, Revisit).

How it renders

Colour washes, ink and underlines use the CSS Custom Highlight API: the book’s DOM is never touched, so pagination and CFIs stay stable. Bold is drawn as a hairline text stroke, so nothing reflows. Italic changes glyph shapes, so it wraps the text in a marked span that CFI generation skips (as epub.js does) and the page re-measures. Exports use the W3C/Readium annotation format: the 6 colours map to pink, orange, yellow, green, blue and purple, plus pubre’s own styles.

Hard words, offline

A word is “hard” when its English frequency is below Zipf 3 (once per million words, from wordfreq) and it isn’t a name. Definitions come from Open English WordNet (CC-BY), sharded by first letters and loaded only when you hover. An online lookup is opt-in and labelled as leaving the device. The definitions in this demo are hand-written samples.

Lenses compose

The guide, lenses and your highlights stack: the cursor rides under a tinted name inside your yellow highlight, and none of them fight. Each lens is one toggle in the menu, remembered per book. A later lens, Parts of speech (Immersive Reader style), loads a ~1 MB tagger only when switched on.

Part III · continued

Richness without clutter

Marks should add richness, never noise. Three mechanisms keep it that way. An attention budget allows each intensity a number of marks per 100 words, and the most valuable win. Focus scoping makes the paragraph you’re reading rich and keeps everything else calm. Graceful collapse means a dense pattern shrinks to its minimal form instead of flooding the page.

Edge cases and what pubre does

Dialogue-heavy page (more than 60% of a paragraph is speech, e.g. ch. 31)Speech collapses to tinted quote marks “ ” in the speaker’s colour. The separation stays visible and the words stay ink.
Name-dense text (cast lists, Russian novels, the Extracts)Only the first mention per paragraph is tinted; the rest are held. The guide reveals each one as it reaches it.
Archaic or technical prose (ch. 32: Latin, cetology)Only the rarest words (lowest Zipf) within the budget are underlined, and at most one foreign phrase per paragraph. Everything else reveals on hover.
One word carries many meanings (a name, inside your highlight, under the pointer, in a quote)Channels never collide (ink, underline, wash and pointer are separate layers). At most two system marks per word, in the order yours › pointer › who › tell-me-more › pattern.
Very long sentences (40+ words, often Melville)Focus narrows to the clause between punctuation, so the contrast still points somewhere useful.
Verse, headings, tables, code, links, footnote referencesLenses skip them. Links and note references are already “tell me more” underlines in the grammar.
Low-contrast themes (Quiet, Nord, Dracula)Dim levels compress so dimmed text never drops below 3:1 contrast with the page.
Small screensThe budget counts words on screen, not paragraphs. At Guided, hard-word underlines become dwell-only on phones.
The author’s own formatting (italics, small caps, bold)Never overridden. Your bold adds a hairline stroke; your italic wraps a span.
Characters you haven’t met yetNot tinted until their first mention; counts stop at your page.
Guide offNo dimming. Lenses run at the ambient budget.

Writing patterns

  • Speech: the speaker’s ink. Attribution from “said Stubb” / “Stubb whispered”, otherwise inherited from the previous speaker or the narration.
  • Asides (parentheses, paired dashes): one contrast step quieter, like a lowered voice.
  • Foreign phrases: a dashed underline, with a translation on dwell.
  • Letters, verse, songs, epigraphs: block-level, handled by chapter stages (Part V).
  • Later, Rich only: recurring motifs (“white”), measures and dates converted on dwell, rhetorical questions.

Budget per 100 words

  • Quiet: 0. Only yours and the pointer.
  • Guided: about 2.5 (at least 1 per paragraph), doubled in the paragraph you’re guiding.
  • Rich: about 5, up to 3 foreign phrases, and every motion verb.

Value order: first mention of a name, then the rarest hard word, then foreign phrases. Speech and asides don’t spend the budget: they recolour existing ink rather than adding marks.

    How it’s built

    One insights engine, one rule

    A new src/insights/ module, UI-free like the EPUB protocol layer. It reads chapter text once in a Web Worker, writes a BookProfile to IndexedDB, and answers every query with a position. Anything past your high-water mark comes back as shape only: length, fingerprint, time. Never names, moods or words.
