Motion rules every pick follows

From the motion research (onset capture, reading under animation, look-back studies). These apply to everything below, whatever you tick.

Only the pointer moves on the line you're reading. Nothing scales, blinks, pulses, glides or reflows there. Animation beside text adds fixations and regressions (Simola 2011).
Ambient means slow and small. Weather and glows cycle over 6–10 s and change lightness by 3–6% at most, never on the text itself.
Steps are quick. A pointer step takes 100–160 ms with an ease-out and no overshoot. Page turns take 300–450 ms on one axis, follow your finger, and can be cancelled.
Springs are for buttons, not pages. Delight motion (200–350 ms, a small bounce) lives in chrome you tapped, never in the book.
Endings settle, they don't loom. Closure moments (a chapter end) run 600–1200 ms once, receding instead of growing toward you.
Read text stays readable. Hiding what you just read hurts comprehension (Schotter 2014), so read text dims but never disappears.

Turning and scrolling

Today a page slides with a fixed 300-odd ms ease and bounces at the book's ends. These make the motion feel physical, or offer a calmer or continuous alternative. Ticking two or more of M2–M4 adds a "Page turn" row to Appearance.

Setting the type

Many free EPUBs are typed like plain text files: straight quotes, double hyphens, spaced paragraphs, ragged bottoms. Since the text is ours to set, we can typeset it like a printed book.

The weather of the passage

Today pubre gives a whole chapter one faint weather trace (storm, fog, snow, night). This takes it down to the passage: as you turn pages, or as the Stage moves paragraph to paragraph, the background quietly follows what the text describes. It must stay a touch you barely notice, not an effect you watch. Try the three strengths on the specimens below. The lines are samples written for this page.

The pointer: what research says, and where to take it

Today the pointer hops word to word in one of nine shapes (ring by default), with ink brightening behind it. Below are the findings that should shape it, then the variants, then four directions that bundle them. Tick variants, and pick one direction.

No study shows a pointer makes adults read faster. Pacer and ruler claims come from vendors and patents. Sell it as place-keeping and focus, not speed. PS Mag
Going back must stay cheap. When readers can't look back (RSVP, Spritz), comprehension drops. Schotter, Tran & Rayner 2014. Our look-back rail is the right instinct. Psych. Science
The eye reads ahead. Readers take in the next word or two before looking at them (parafoveal preview), and skip about a third of short words. Our "ahead" text sits at 20% brightness, which likely blurs that preview. Stepping on every "the" and "of" is not how eyes move.
Bolded word starts don't help. Readwise timed 2,074 readers: Bionic Reading was 2.6 wpm slower, so no effect. Skip fixation-letter gimmicks. Readwise
Line changes are where readers get lost. BeeLine's colour gradients target the return sweep, but the evidence is thin and mostly from the vendor. Short lines did help dyslexic readers (Schneps 2013). Behavioral Scientist
